I’ve always found the act of reflection powerful. That’s what motivated me to create my GitHub repo, [cigarettes](https://github.com/justin-napolitano/cigarettes). This isn’t your standard coding project; it’s a personal journal capturing thoughts and moments revolving around lifestyle and anxiety, with a specific focus on the theme of smoking cigarettes. 

## Why This Project Exists

There’s something innately human about journaling. It allows me to unpack feelings and experiences that often get buried in daily chaos. The topic of smoking, despite its negatives, holds a certain nostalgia and poignancy for me. I think many people can relate to using cigarettes as a symbol—whether that's tranquility, rebellion, or anxiety.

I wanted a space to document this exploration—not just to share with others, but to reflect on my own journey. It’s a deeply personal project, and creating it was an emotional process. 

## Key Design Decisions

Creating this repo was all about simplicity. I wanted to keep it text-based and straightforward. Here’s how I approached it:

- **Markdown Format:** I chose markdown for its ease of use. It allows me to format my thoughts clearly without any fuss. Plus, markdown files are a breeze to read and edit.
- **Static Content:** There’s no heavy framework here. Just plain-text files. This approach keeps things lightweight and avoids the complexity that often comes with web apps.
- **Focused Themes:** I wanted to hone in on specific themes—lifestyle, anxiety, and personal introspection. This lets me dig deeper into my experiences and document them authentically.

## Tech Stack

The tech stack is as simple as it gets:

- **Markdown:** For all content formatting; no frills, just text.
- **Static Files:** The entire project is based on markdown files—very minimal overhead.

The focus here isn’t on flashy tech but rather on the substance of what I’m writing.
